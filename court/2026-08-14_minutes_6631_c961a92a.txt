GRIMES COUNTY COMMISSIONERS COURT

SPECIAL MEETING
OFFICIAL MINUTES — FRIDAY, AUGUST 14, 2026
The Grimes County Commissioners Court met on Friday, August 14, 2026, at 9:00 A.M. Members
Present: County Judge Joe Fauth, Commissioner Chad Mallett, Commissioner David Tullos,
Commissioner Michael Raska, Commissioner Phillip Cox. County Judge Joe Fauth called the meeting to
order.

1. Discuss and review the Grimes County FY 2027 proposed budget. — Joe Fauth Ill

After reading the agenda items, County Judge Joe Fauth stated that Public Comment Forms
have been submitted and Statute 551.007 of the Texas Government Code does allow that the
Public has statutory right to address a government body on an item posted on the agenda.
Commissioner David Tullos requested a Point of Order. Mr Tullos stated he consulted with the
County Attorney to ensure the Court was in compliance with Open Meetings Act, because there
was not notification allowing Public Comments through the published agenda, therefore, Public
Comments are not allowed at this meeting. Mr Tullos further stated that these individuals can be
recognized to speak during the discussion on the budget itself. Judge Fauth, once again, stated
Statute 551.007. Statutory rights requires a government body to allow public testimony on items
listed on an open meeting agenda. Commissioner Phillip Cox stated that the Judge referenced it
as Public Comments. This is a budget discussion and they are treated differently. The
Commissioners Court engaged in extensive discussion of the Grimes County FY 2027 proposed
budget, with multiple county employees and officials addressing compensation disparities and
budget priorities. Chris Accord, justice of the peace for Precinct 1, spoke on behalf of his clerical
staff, noting that he had sought raises for his employees for three years without success. He
referenced Judge Fauth's proposed balanced budget that included a $4 per hour raise for all
employees with no additional tax burden on taxpayers, and he urged the court to approve it,
observing that Commissioners' salaries had increased by 20.7 percent over the past three years
while hourly employees had received far less. Commissioner David Tullos challenged Accord's
characterization, noting that justices of the peace had also solicited raises in previous budget
hearings and that Accord's own salary increase exceeded what the Commissioners had
received. Commissioner Phillip Cox disputed whether a balanced budget had been submitted,
characterizing it as incomplete and deficit by millions of dollars, though Judge Fauth clarified that
the proposed budget included $2 million in contingency funds from SpaceX money that could
address much of the shortfall. Additional county employees addressed the court, emphasizing
that a $4 per hour raise should apply county-wide rather than to law enforcement alone. Jamie
Weeks, the 911 addressing coordinator, highlighted the county's rapid growth driven by the
TeraFab project and the workload demands on county staff, urging investment in all employees
to retain experienced workers and attract qualified applicants. Chief of Staff Patti Ermis
presented a petition signed by over 100 county employees requesting equitable pay increases
across all departments, arguing that inflation affects all workers equally and that differentiated
raises fracture morale and undervalue non-law-enforcement staff. Benjamin Napier of the Road
and Bridge department, Richard Gore, an assistant construction supervisor, Crystal Jarvis,
executive assistant to Judge Tuck McClain, and IT Director Gregg Cannon all spoke in support of

county-wide raises. Kirk Cloyd, development services technician, referenced the 2023

classification and compensation study by H&R compensation consultants that had identified pay

disparities and noted that Grimes County's impending growth would make competitive

compensation essential for retention. Elections Administrator Rachel Walker supported law

and retention difficult. County Attorney Megan Barcak and County Auditor Jessi Murphy

to all employees required to hold a TCOL certification, including dispatchers, jailers, and deputy

recruitment concerns, and noted that deputies had expressed they would leave if offered higher-paying
positions elsewhere. Commissioner Phillip Cox emphasized that law enforcement

personnel who carry weapons and face greater occupational hazards deserve different

compensation consideration than administrative staff, and the court discussed whether certain

non-TCOL-certified employees in the district attorney's office and others should be included in

any pay adjustment. The discussion extended to early voting locations, SB 22 fund usage, and

various technical budget matters, with Commissioner David Tullos advocating for a no-new-revenue

tax rate while still addressing employee compensation where possible.

Action: Motion by Commissioner David Tullos to Recess until the county attorney can be
present to address the Open Meetings Act question in person so the Court doesn't
knowingly violate the Open Meetings Act. Seconded by Commissioner Phillip Cox Failed
(2-3-0).

Voting Yea: Commissioner David Tullos, Commissioner Phillip Cox

Voting Nay: Commissioner Chad Mallett, Commissioner Michael Raska, County Judge Joe
Fauth

2. Consider and take action to propose a tax rate and take a record vote on proposed tax
rate. If proposed tax rate will exceed the voter-approval tax rate or the no new revenue tax
rate (whichever is lower), take record vote and schedule a public hearing. — Joe Fauth Ill

County Judge Joe Fauth introduced the tax rate discussion and explained that the proposed
budget process had created a chicken-and-egg situation, as he lacked certified revenue numbers
when preparing his initial proposal. County Auditor Jessi Murphy requested that budget
discussion be deferred until after the tax rate was established. County Judge Fauth moved to
propose a tax rate of 0.40, representing a reduction from the current rate of 0.423077, along with
specific employee salary recommendations; however, County Attorney Megan Barcak clarified
that the motion for tax rate needed to address only the rate itself. Commissioner David Tullos
proposed an alternative rate of 0.39, which failed on a vote. Tullos then moved for a compromise
rate of 0.395, seconded by Commissioner Cox. During extensive discussion before the vote,
County Judge Fauth presented comparative salary data from other Texas counties showing that
Grimes County's compensation packages were competitive or below average, and advocated for
meaningful salary increases. Commissioner Chad Mallett raised concerns about capital project
funding and the need to reserve adequate funds from the expected ten-million-dollar payment.
Commissioner Phillip Cox questioned the methodology and noted prior issues with merit-based
pay distribution. County Attorney Megan Barcak and others stressed the importance of

revenue and how the county could potentially recapture unused tax capacity within three years.
GCAD Shannon Sanders was called to the podium to explain the mechanics of foregone

Action: Motion by Judge Joe Fauth to propose a 2027 tax rate decrease from the current
rate of 0.423077 to 0.40, showing a tax rate reduction of 0.023077. No second. Motion failed.

valuation. Seconded by Commissioner Phillip Cox. Failed (2-3-0)

Voting Yea: Commissioner David Tullos, Commissioner Phillip Cox

Fauth

Action: Motion by Commissioner David Tullos to propose a 2027 tax rate of 0.395 per $100

Raska, County Judge Joe Fauth
Voting Nay: Commissioner Chad Mallett

A motion by Commissioner David Tullos to adjourn the meeting was made, seconded by Commissioner
Michael Raska. Carried. The meeting adjourned at 11:42 AM.

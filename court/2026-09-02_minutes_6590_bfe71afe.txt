The Grimes County Commissioners Court met on Wednesday, September 2, 2026, at 8:30 A.M. Members

Commissioner Phillip Cox.

County Judge Joe Fauth called the meeting to order.

  Grimes County Judge - Joe Fauth III
  County Judge Joe Fauth opened the public hearing on the Grimes County 2026-2027 proposed budget
  and noted that Commissioner Mallett was absent but a quorum was present. The judge invited public

Public Hearing
    1  Consider and hear public comments regarding the Grimes County 2026/2027 proposed
      budget, pursuant to Texas Local Government Code, Chapter 111, Section 111.007.
       Pursuant to Texas Local Government Code, Chapter 111, Section 111.007.
       County Judge Joe Fauth opened the public hearing on the Grimes County 2026/2027 proposed
       budget and invited comments from citizens. Debra Morwood noted positive steps including the
       addition of a fire marshal position and increased investment in fire protection, emergency
       management, and ambulances, but raised concerns about whether the county was planning far
       enough ahead for industrial growth. She asked whether the fire marshal had conducted a
       long-range assessment of needed infrastructure such as hydrants, fire flow capacity, water
       storage, and staffing, and whether any of the budgeted ambulances were intended for the
       ambulance bay required under the SpaceX tax abatement agreement. Karen Shiver expressed
       appreciation for changes to the election office and continued support for libraries and food banks,
       and suggested the court develop a salary increase formula that prioritizes lower-paid workers over
       higher earners rather than giving percentage raises across the board. Marie Egyed, representing
       the Grimes County Citizens for Responsible Development, acknowledged the budget's increased
       funding for road materials and bridge work but expressed concern that staffing levels in
       development management, permitting, inspections, and 911 services were not increasing
       proportionally to handle the anticipated economic growth from major industrial projects; she also
       raised frustration that the current budget posted online differed from what the court was
       considering, preventing meaningful public comment. County Judge Fauth responded to several
       points: he confirmed the court's intent to hire a dedicated fire marshal and explained the
       September 16 meeting was planned for the final budget vote; he noted the court had proposed
       raising the minimum salary to twenty dollars per hour; he addressed ambulance procurement,
       explaining that four new ambulances ordered would eventually replace aging high-mileage units
       and that TeraFab would provide its own ambulance at its facility; and he discussed the timing of
       TeraFab payments, clarifying that an early $10 million payment in 2027 would help fund needed
       personnel and materials. County Attorney Megan Barcak explained that the budget posted online
       was the version approved on August 14 per statutory requirement and that subsequent revisions
       made in public meetings had not been reposted pending finalization by the auditor's office. Karen
       Shiver added a personal account of a recent death in Bedias that highlighted ambulance
       availability concerns and expressed the need for additional ambulances, to which the judge
       responded by explaining county mutual aid agreements with Bryan-College Station and

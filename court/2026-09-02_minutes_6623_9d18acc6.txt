5  Public Comments
  Marie Egyed, representing Grimes County citizens for responsible development from Iola, thanked the
  court for listening to concerns presented on August 5 and noted two petitions with almost 700 and 900
  signatures respectively requesting better communication and specific protections for Grimes County.
  She stated that despite the judge's draft resolution being tabled twice to incorporate public input, the
  county still lacks concrete measurable protections nearly two months after the resolution was first
  presented, and asked the court to complete the resolution with the same urgency shown in passing the
  tariff fab deal and bring it back for a vote.
  Debra Morwood thanked development services for providing a spreadsheet of submitted projects but
  noted that residents continue to observe construction and development activity not reflected on that
  spreadsheet, creating uncertainty and stress. She requested that the county improve direct
  communication with residents by answering reasonable questions without requiring lengthy attorney
  general processes, stating that residents need to know what is coming and what protections are in
  place to make informed decisions about their property and futures.

  of Commerce is holding a "Taste of Grimes County" at the Expo Center Thursday evening. Doors 5:00,
  no later than 5:30, running to roughly 7:30 or 8:00 or until the food runs out. Entry is $25, a number of
  area restaurants and food establishments are participating, and the chamber makes charitable

7  Presentation of Monthly Departmental Reports
    7  Human Resources, IT, Elections — Human Resources, IT, Elections
       Human Resource Director Atina Adair reported on current staffing vacancies across county
       departments. The sheriff's office has four patrol vacancies, one jailer position with a candidate
       processing to start Monday, one dispatcher vacancy, and a vacant jail administrator position. Road
       and Bridge is fully staffed with four positions filled through the Ameritech temp agency that are
       performing well, with at least one expected to transition to permanent full-time status within weeks.
       County Judge Joe Fauth inquired whether the temporary employees would remain after their
       assignments concluded, and Director Adair confirmed positive feedback from the road department
       regarding their performance and attendance. She noted visiting the department several times
       weekly and observing strong engagement from the workforce. The auditor's office has one
       assistant auditor position available, with several interviews completed and second-round
       interviews planned within the week. IT Director Gregg Cannon then provided an extensive
       technology report, noting an increasing number of cyberattacks against the county network,
       particularly AI-driven attacks that exploit security vulnerabilities within seconds of disclosure. He
       reported assigning staff to conduct monthly penetration testing rather than annually, which has
       already identified and corrected security flaws. The past month marked the first time in several
       months with 100 percent uptime and no cybersecurity incidents, with the network blocking
       approximately 19,000 emails. Infrastructure health remains strong at 99 percent uptime, though
       storage utilization has crept to 73.1 percent capacity. County Judge Fauth confirmed that budget
       provisions exist for additional storage capacity to prevent performance degradation.

9  Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Agents
    9  Quarterly Interpretation. — Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Agents
       AgriLife Matt Pfeifer provided a comprehensive update on agricultural and natural resource
       programming in Grimes County. He reported on recent workshops, including a feral hog workshop
       hosted in partnership with Madison County that drew fifteen participants and seven volunteers,
       and a small ruminant educational program covering parasite management and screwworm
       concerns. Looking ahead, AgriLife agents from eighteen counties across District 9 will visit Grimes
       County on September 16 and 17 for professional development tours showcasing local agricultural
       production. The new program year began September 1, with plans to continue the South Central
       Texas new landowner program for individuals who have inherited or purchased property, and to
       host a cow-calf clinic in October focused on beef cattle production. Master gardeners
       demonstrated strong activity during the past program year, hosting 48 educational events with
       over 1,100 attendees and contributing more than 2,000 volunteer hours. On the youth side,
       AgriLife sent six Grimes County 4-H members to Texas 4-H Congress, a mock legislative event
       where participants take on roles as elected officials and submit bills to gain firsthand experience
       with the legislative process at the state capital. The fairgrounds and expo received recognition with
       a Friend of 4-H award for their support of programming. Additionally, Matt Pfeifer briefly noted
       ongoing programming in health, wellness, nutrition, car seat inspections, and community outreach,
       including a back-to-school event that distributed over 900 backpacks to Grimes County students.

       with the tree company handling the planting.

    15  Discuss and take action to provide requested additional funds to the Grimes County Jail to
       cover Inmate Food and Inmate Medical expenses resulting from a projected shortfall in the
       FY 2026 budget. — Martha Smith, Grimes County Sheriff's Office
       Chief Deputy Martha Smith and Michelle Weaks presented the Grimes County Jail's request for
       additional funding to cover projected shortfalls in inmate food and medical expenses for the
       remainder of the fiscal year. Michelle Weaks explained that the jail had originally budgeted for
       between ninety and ninety-three inmates but was currently housing nearly 100 or more, with the
       population at 103 that week, necessitating an additional $20,000 for inmate food. She further
       detailed that professional services through the Southern Health Partners contract, which covers
       prescriptions and over-the-counter items, had a $12,000 monthly cap, and clarified a budgeting
       misunderstanding regarding how those costs were calculated. For inmate medical expenses, an
       additional $20,000 was requested due to exceptionally high medical bills, including one inmate

    20  Discuss and take action on any issue regarding FY 27 Proposed Budget. — Joe Fauth III
       County Judge Joe Fauth initiated discussion of the FY 2027 proposed budget, noting that the court
       had received a revised budget document and had several days to review it. County Auditor Jessi
       Murphy presented numerous changes made to the budget since the previous version. She
       reported updates to deputy constable pay scales to include the minimum brought up to the
       mid-range, pay scale adjustments for the district attorney's office totaling $3,000 for step
       increases, and adjustments for constables ranging from $1,500 to zero depending on tenure.
       Murphy noted the addition of an ambulance subsidy, a reduction in patrol positions from four to
       three with one investigator added at grade one, and a reduction in emergency operations
       coordinators from three to two. She reported that interest earned revenue was increased to
       $1,100,000 and flagged postage budget line 4310 as requiring discussion. County Attorney Megan
       Barcak clarified that the county was experiencing a significant increase in public information act
       requests, which involved substantial expenses for attorney general consultations. She explained
       the need to separate postage expenses for CPS cases from postage used for PIA requests,
       proposing to reduce the postage line to $400 while creating a new PIA expenses line budgeted at
       $800, representing a net increase of $400 to her office budget. Barcak reported that PIA requests
       had grown from approximately 74 at the time of her budget hearing to 110, with more pending, and
       indicated that many requests required attorney general opinions due to potential liability concerns.
       Murphy also presented capital projects broken down by department, including $1,500,000 for
       justice of the peace construction and $9,600,000 for jail renovation and construction. She detailed
       various requested changes to the historical commission budget, reallocating funds among
       expense lines and increasing amounts for repairs and maintenance, advertising, and water
       expenses. Murphy reported that the current budget maintained a balanced position while drawing
       approximately $11,500,000 from reserves. The discussion at length addressed multiple aspects of

       with County Judge Fauth noting potential for increased revenue based on recent activity levels and
       suggesting the auditor conduct a mid-week reassessment of revenue figures. The matter of a

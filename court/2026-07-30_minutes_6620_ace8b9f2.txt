GRIMES COUNTY BUDGET HEARINGS
OFFICIAL MINUTES — THURSDAY, JULY 30, 2026

The Grimes County Budget Hearings met on Thursday, July 30, 2026, at 9:00 A.M. Members Present:
County Judge Joe Fauth, Commissioner Chad Mallett, Commissioner David Tullos, Commissioner Michael
Raska, Commissioner Phillip Cox.

County Judge Joe Fauth called the meeting to order.

Discuss and review the Grimes County FY 2027 departmental budget requests. — Joe Fauth Ill

County Judge Joe Fauth opened the departmental budget review meeting and provided an overview of
the proposed budget, which included a $4 per hour increase for all employees including elected
officials and department heads, with minimums of $15 per hour for temporary employees, $20 per hour
for part-time employees, and $20 per hour for full-time employees. The court reviewed budgets for
multiple departments throughout the day, beginning with the District Clerk's office. Diane LeFlore
District Clerk requested consideration for salary adjustments to bring her office closer in alignment with
similar county positions, noting she had lost five dedicated employees to other offices partly due to
compensation gaps. She also addressed overtime needs, citing upcoming trials, and discussed
supplies, postage, and dues renewal fees. County Judge Joe Fauth acknowledged the complexity of
comparing salaries across departments with different funding sources, particularly those with Senate
Bill 22 funding. The discussion proceeded through operational expenses including office supplies,
postage, and various line items, with adjustments made based on current spending patterns and
anticipated needs. The court also examined the IT department's extensive software budget of
approximately $1.2 million, which County Judge Joe Fauth noted was necessary for county operations
despite its size. IT Director Gregg Cannon presented numerous software requests and discussed
cybersecurity initiatives, including multi-factor authentication and email protection measures, while
addressing concerns raised by Commissioner Phillip Cox about monitoring software usage to ensure
efficiency. The court reviewed requests from the Emergency Management and Fire Marshal office,
including equipment needs and personnel requests for growth management. County Treasurer Tom
Maynard presented the treasurer's budget, which included discussion of a new timekeeping software
system, and the Tax Assessor/Collector Mary Ann Waters presented her budget with requests for
equipment replacement and records preservation. The court also discussed Human Resources office
needs and a proposed title change for a senior administrative assistant to executive assistant with
responsibilities as benefits coordinator. Substantial time was devoted to the Road Department budget
under County Engineer Jon Steiber's direction, which included discussion of restructuring
environmental permits and inspections as a separate department, moving certain personnel, requesting
additional development technicians to handle growth in residential and commercial development, and
addressing equipment needs including vehicles and bridge projects. The court examined grant-funded
projects, capital improvements, and discussed the aging equipment fleet requiring replacement. County
Auditor Jessi Murphy provided a summary of changes recommended and confirmed tax rate
information, noting preliminary calculations for the no-new-revenue rate and voter approval rate, with
plans to provide detailed information for court review before the August 7 rate-setting meeting.

Presentation of Monthly Departmental Reports:

9. Human Resources, IT, Elections — Human Resources, IT, Elections

Human Resource Director Atina Adair reported on current vacancies across Grimes County. The
Sheriff's Office has six patrol deputy positions open, two recently hired cadets, and two to three
more candidates in background processing. Three jailer positions are vacant following two in-house
promotions to sergeant positions effective October 1 and the movement of one jailer to
patrol. Three dispatcher positions are also open with candidates processing. In the Road
Department, one operator position and one administrative assistant position are open, with over
20 candidates filtered for the administrative role and interviews scheduled to begin the following
week. One development tech position is also vacant. The EMC and Fire Marshal's office has one
fire marshal deputy position open. All positions are being sourced through online postings, social
media, Ameritech Staffing, and the Texas Workforce Commission.

IT Director Gregg Cannon reported 100 percent uptime across Internet and network connections
with no cybersecurity incidents or major outages. A new phishing testing system is in place to
assess employee compliance with security guidelines, and endpoint protection through Cybernet
is now fully operational with improved network visibility. Patch management compliance stands
at 85 percent across approximately 250 workstations and thirty-five servers. The new backup
system is operational with no ransomware alerts, and infrastructure storage and service uptime
exceeds 99.99 percent. Storage utilization is at 73 percent, and an approved replacement
system will arrive within months at a revised cost savings of approximately $40,000. The camera
system operates at 99 percent capacity with 177 cameras; additional external cameras for Road
and Bridge are planned. The help desk processed approximately 140 closed tickets of routine
daily matters from 150 open tickets. Multi-factor authentication and fingerprint sign-in for patrol
officers are planned for rollout within two months. The jail archiving project is progressing with
scanning of approximately 150 boxes of sheriff's office records beginning that week.

Elections Administrator Rachel Walker reported an extraordinarily busy voter registration period.
The office processed approximately 700 DPS applications, 63 mail-in and walk-in applications,
and 35 Texas online applications in the current month, along with approximately 45 ballot-by-mail
applications and 15 mailed applications. On September 3, the office received approximately 784
voter registration records from a statewide DPS system issue involving incomplete voter
registration transactions dating back to October 2025. The backlog required careful individual
review to prevent duplicate records and ensure accurate updates, prompting the hiring of three
temporary employees beginning September 28 to assist after completion of secretary of state
cyber training and hands-on instruction. The office worked extended hours, including weekends,
to process the backlog while managing normal voter registration workload and the October 5
registration deadline. Additionally, the Elections Department successfully partnered with
Anderson Shire Elementary School on September 24 to conduct a student council election using
county voting equipment, with 138 fourth and fifth graders participating and two third graders
serving as election clerks operating the controller and scanner. The event received
overwhelmingly positive feedback from parents, teachers, staff, and school board members.

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension — JF, Texas A&M AgriLife Extension

11. Presentation and approval of National 4-H Week Proclamation. — JF, Texas A&M AgriLife
Extension

Matt Pfeifer, Grimes County Extension Agent for Agriculture and Natural Resources, presented a
National 4-H Week Proclamation for Commissioners Court approval. Pfeifer explained that
National 4-H Week is observed across the country as an opportunity for 4-H members to thank
their communities for supporting the program. He reported that Grimes County finished the year
with approximately 238 4-H members and 65 registered adult volunteers. Pfeifer noted that the
week would culminate in a Saturday community service event in partnership with the Grimes
County Animal Shelter, where 4-H members would provide exercise for shelter animals. He
introduced several 4-H council members who were present to speak about their experiences in
the organization. Colton Whitaker, vice president of the Grimes County 4-H Council, Becca
Butler, second vice president, Cameron Colson, Kaitlin Colson, Faith Shreve, and Jenna Journey
each shared remarks about their 4-H involvement and projects, which included robotics,
livestock, shooting sports, photography, and baking. Several members described their
participation in Texas 4-H Congress, a mock legislative event held that summer where they
served in the House of Representatives, wrote bills, attended legislative sessions, and learned
about the governmental process. Pfeifer then read the proclamation aloud, which highlighted 4-H
as America's largest youth development organization, noted its role in building a ready
generation equipped with skills for the future, and recognized its significance in preparing young
people for work and life through experiential learning.

Focusing Families - Hilda Martinez — Hilda Martinez, Focusing Families

12. Presentation and approval of the Proclamation designating the month of October 2026 as

Domestic Violence Awareness Month and authorize the County Judge as signatory. — Hilda
Martinez, Focusing Families

Hilda Martinez, representing Focusing Families, presented the Proclamation designating October
2026 as Domestic Violence Awareness Month. Martinez shared the story of Dulce Martinez, a
mother of four from Hempstead who was murdered in October 2022 by her abusive partner Aldo
Ramirez after enduring stalking, physical abuse, and control. She noted that Ramirez had a
pattern of abuse in previous relationships and was sentenced to life in prison. Martinez
highlighted that Focusing Families provided 2,194 safe shelter nights and answered 771 crisis
hotline calls, yet emphasized that the most critical figure remains those currently suffering in
silence behind closed doors. She read the proclamation text, which acknowledged that domestic
violence affects all aspects of a community and disproportionately impacts women and children,
violating their rights to live with respect, dignity, and freedom from fear. The proclamation
committed the county to ending domestic violence through public education, victim services, and
cooperation with government officials, law enforcement, health professionals, social service
providers, educators, clergy, and community leaders. Commissioner Phillip Cox presented the
item for approval with County Judge as signatory. Commissioner Chad Mallett confirmed receipt
of the motion and asked for those in favor.

Action: Motion by Commissioner Phillip Cox to Approve the Proclamation designating October
2026 as Domestic Violence Awareness Month and authorize the County Judge as signatory.
Seconded by Commissioner Michael Raska. Carried (4—0—0-1).

CONTRACT FOR SERVICES

WHEREAS Grimes County, Texas hereinafter COUNTY, a political subdivision of the State of
Texas, has the authority, under Texas Agriculture Code, 201.152 to contribute funds to a conservation
district for flood control and drainage

IT IS THEREFORE AGREED THAT:

1. The Bedias Creek Soil and Water Conservation District #428 will provide services to the residents

of COUNTY as follows:
a. Respond to individuals and groups requesting technical aid concerning natural resources.
b. Work with the commissioner court, as requested, on soil and water problems.
c. Assist with dry hydrant design and installation.
d. Help with design work concerning water flow and culvert design.
e. Work with emergency programs, such as Emergency Watershed Protection and disaster
programs.
TERM

THE TERM OF THIS AGREEMENT is one year beginning on January 1, 2027 and ending on
December 31, 2027; unless earlier terminated by either party on thirty (30) days written notice.

CONSIDERATION

COUNTY agrees to pay Bedias Creek Soil and Water Conservation District #428 the sum of
$1000.00 for the provision of the services described above.

Payment shall be made on or after January 1, 2027, and BEFORE March 1, 2027

THIS AGREEMENT is effective on the date it is executed by Bedias Creek Soil and Water
Conservation District #428 or upon acceptance, by order, of the Grimes County Commissioners Court,
whichever date is later in time.

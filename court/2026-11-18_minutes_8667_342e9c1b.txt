6. Elections, Veterans Services, Emergency Management Services

Elections Administrator Rachel Walker reported that Grimes County currently has 22,319
registered voters. She provided detailed statistics from the recent election held in
October, noting that early voting ran from October 19 through the 30th at two locations:
the historical courthouse in Anderson, which was open for twelve days with 123 public
voting hours, and the Navasota Center, open for eleven days with 102 public voting
hours. On election day, the county operated 11 polling locations across Grimes County,
each open from 7 AM to 7 PM, and benefited from eleven student election clerks from
lowa High School who assisted with daily operations and technology support. The
election drew 11,896 participants out of 22,240 eligible voters, representing
approximately 53.49 percent turnout. Early in-person voting accounted for 7,536 voters
with 7,534 ballots counted, while election day saw 3,896 voters with 3,895 ballots cast.
An additional 426 voters participated by mail, though only 393 of those ballots were
accepted and counted. County Judge Joe Fauth asked what causes some mailed ballots
to be rejected, and Walker explained that signature mismatches and personally
identifiable information issues are the primary reasons, with voters given an opportunity
to correct deficiencies if they submit corrections timely. Additionally, ballots not received
by the statutory deadline are automatically rejected by the ballot board. Of the 38
provisional ballots submitted, 12 were accepted after investigation, bringing the total
accepted ballots to 11,834. Walker noted that staffing included 78 total personnel across
all shifts, with 35 unique workers covering early voting and 60 people assigned to
election day duties. The post-election hand count audit, recently completed by an eight-
member ballot board, involved hand counting 8,670 ballots over eleven hours, covering
ballots from both early voting locations and all absentee ballots, plus randomly selected
bags from other locations. The audit examined three races—the United States Senate
race, the chief justice Supreme Court race, and the county commissioner Precinct 4 race
—and produced zero discrepancies, confirming that hand counts matched machine
results exactly. Walker announced that the county would conduct its first statewide risk-
limiting audit on November 19, a new requirement implemented by the state and
conducted simultaneously across all 254 Texas counties, using statistically selected
paper ballots to verify reported election results. Because Grimes County had cast only
about 11,000 ballots out of over 9 million statewide, Walker anticipated the county would
need to pull only one or two ballots for the statewide audit. County Judge Fauth
confirmed that the court had canvassed the election results last Thursday and submitted
them to the state. Walker also reported that the Elections office is preparing for the
arrival of a new employee expected to begin on November 30, following a successful
offer process, and is adjusting office procedures and infrastructure to accommodate a
three-person department.

Veterans Services Coordinator Jay Lafferty reported that October was lighter than
average, with 29 phone contacts, seven office contacts, and four email contacts. He
initiated one new claim and reviewed another existing claim. Lafferty also conducted one
jail visit with a veteran held in the Grimes County Jail. He noted that his department's
informational banner was displayed at the Patriots Texas event, which drew

11. Discuss and take action on the Grimes County Fire Marshal office implementing an Open
Burn Permit. — Dione Bumpous, Grimes County EMC/Fire Marshal

County Judge Joe Fauth opened the discussion by clarifying that the Commissioners Court had
no prior authority to discuss this matter among themselves before the meeting, as doing so
would violate the Texas Open Meetings Act. He explained that the idea had been placed on the
agenda to allow the court and public to discuss concerns raised by citizens who had contacted
the fire marshal's office and his office about illegal burns. He emphasized that no decision had
been made by the court to implement a permit for yard burning or brush pile burning.
Commissioner David Tullos challenged the framing of this restriction, noting that the Texas Open
Meetings Act does not apply to department heads or conversations with individual
commissioners. He expressed concern that the agenda item itself was poorly communicated,
suggesting a permit for all burns across the entire county, when commissioners should have
received detailed backup information when the agenda was submitted the previous week. He
stated he had received backup materials only that morning after arriving at court, and that a
phone call with Dione Bumpous had confirmed the item described a permit for all burns,
including leaves and debris across the entire county.

EMC/Fire Marshal Dione Bumpous clarified that the recommendation was not for a permit but
rather a controlled burn notification system using the county's existing permitting website. She
explained that the system would automatically notify dispatch, fire chiefs, and the fire marshal
when residents voluntarily submitted burn notifications, centralizing communication and reducing
missed notifications. The system would also enable automated alerts for burn bans and red flag
warnings based on lawful notifications already given by the county. Bumpous acknowledged that
the term "permit" had been incorrect and that the core purpose was to provide an online
notification mechanism for large burns, with a point of contact available if neighbors called in
concerns. She noted that the form would include state laws and regulations regarding prohibited
burn materials, helping educate property owners about what could and could not be burned
under TCEQ regulations in effect since 1996.

Commissioner Tullos reported receiving approximately one thousand responses, primarily
through social media, with phone calls, text messages, and emails, and stated that not a single
person supported this as a permit. He acknowledged that a voluntary notification system had
merit and value but expressed concern that reviewing all potential burns with a staff of one could
become cumbersome and costly, potentially requiring additional funding in the future.
Commissioner Phillip Cox noted that voluntary notification could help reduce unnecessary
emergency callouts when neighbors reported perceived illegal burns. He emphasized that the
county should not mandate anything and that burning is part of the agricultural process in a rural
community. County Judge Joe Fauth indicated that the notification would function similarly to
solid waste disposal listings, informing citizens what could and could not be burned, with
individuals responsible for the consequences if they burned prohibited materials.

After discussion, the court agreed to move forward with the notification system as a voluntary
process rather than a mandatory permit, with no fee attached, to be clearly labeled on a
corrected website listing what could and could not be burned according to state law rather than
county law. County Attorney Megan Barcak confirmed the motion and second were in place.
Commissioner Chad Mallett requested that the website wording be revised, and Commissioner
Phillip Cox cautioned that the notification system should not be used punitively against residents
who chose not to participate and were not doing anything illegal. Dione Bumpous committed to
ensuring fire departments understood the voluntary nature of the system and would not interpret
non-notification as grounds for enforcement action.

14. Consider and take action to create employee policy for "call-outs". — Atina Adair, Grimes
County Human Resources

Human Resource Director Atina Adair presented a formal callout policy for Grimes County
employees. She explained that the county currently lacks a written callout policy in its handbook,
creating ongoing confusion and repeated questions from departments about how to handle
emergency after-hours callouts and compensation. Adair noted that in 2023 and 2024, the HR
department consulted with TAC for guidance on structuring a county callout policy, but it was
never formally drafted or adopted. She clarified that the proposed policy does not change current
operations or add new expenses; rather, it formalizes existing practice, which already guarantees
nonexempt employees a two-hour minimum when called out after hours for county business. The
policy establishes consistent rules across all departments, including requirements for prior
supervisor approval, clear definitions of what constitutes an emergency, and prevention of
overtime double-dipping.

County Judge Joe Fauth raised a procedural question about whether the Commissioners Court
would need to approve each callback instance going forward, given that compensation is already
budgeted. He asked whether that authority could instead rest with department heads, who are
the ones making the callout decisions based on the urgency of the situation. Commissioner
Phillip Cox responded that he had confidence in department heads to follow the policy without
requiring court approval for each instance. County Auditor Jessi Murphy then sought clarification
on when emergency pay requests should come before the court, explaining that departments
typically seek approval only when emergency hours do not reach the overtime threshold of forty
hours per week. When emergency hours push total weekly hours above forty, those hours are
automatically paid at time and a half and do not require court approval. County Attorney Megan
Barcak reinforced this distinction, noting that court approval is requested only when departments

15. Road Department Report. — Jon Steiber, Grimes County Engineer

County Engineer Jon Steiber reported on major road projects completed and in progress. The
department has constructed a new cul de sac on Sunbelt Road to allow trucks to turn around as
part of a right of way swap arrangement from several years prior. Second course chip sealing
has been completed on roads 316A, 313, 206, and 2.5 miles of County Road 175. The
department removed and rebuilt Oak Bridge, a project Steiber described as one of the largest
undertaken by county crews. The engineer noted that seal coating work remains weather-
dependent, requiring sustained temperatures of at least 50 degrees and not falling below 60
degrees for 24 to 48 hours to ensure proper asphalt binding. Roads 115, 242, and 306 remain
ready for seal coating once conditions permit. All roads on the current list that were not
completed this year will retain top priority status.

Steiber discussed ongoing challenges with heavy truck damage to County Road 304 and County
Road 102, the latter being jointly administered with Brazos County. County Road 304 required
milling and treatment with Permazyme followed by recompaction, and discussions with the
responsible entity are ongoing. The department is exploring the possibility of designating County
Road 102 as a no-through truck zone pending legal review. Regarding grant projects, the Tawny
Berry project experienced delay but is expected to start soon pending resolution of a contractor-
engineer issue. The 409 Bridge project is scheduled for bidding in the first quarter of 2027 with
construction beginning the following quarter. An extension of Harvey reallocation funds was
approved for the County Road 102 bridge, with construction anticipated for June or July 2027.
Commissioner David Tullos raised concerns about weather impacts on the Tawney Berry Grant
asphalt project, given the 90-day completion window, and requested careful quality oversight.
County Engineer Steiber acknowledged the concern and noted that onsite laboratory oversight
and temperature requirements would govern asphalt installation, and that discussions were
ongoing regarding whether to defer the project to warmer weather conditions.

between Grimes County and the City of Plantersville, Texas, pursuant to
the authority granted and in compliance with the Interlocal Cooperation
Act, Chapter 791, Texas Government Code.

1. Contracting Parties: The Receiving Party (“RECEIVING PARTY") is
the City of Plantersville, Texas. The Performing Party
(“PERFORMING PARTY") is Grimes County, Texas.

rs Statement of Services to be Performed:

(a) Fire Marshal Service

PERFORMING PARTY agrees to provide Fire Marshal services within the
corporate limits of RECEIVING PARTY.

3. Term of Contract: This AGREEMENT shall have a Five-year term, but
the Contract shall be reviewed annually by the Parties to ascertain
its continuing appropriateness.

4. Termination: Either party may, by providing written notice to the
other party thirty (30) days in advance, terminate this AGREEMENT
in whole or in part at any time.

5. Miscellaneous:
(a) The UNDERSIGNED CONTRACTING PARTIES certify that:
(i) the services specified above are necessary and essential
for activities that are properly within the statutory
functions of the affected parties;

(ii) the proposed arrangements serve the interest of efficient
and economical administration of the contracting parties;

(iii) During the term of this agreement, the Mayor of
RECEIVING PARTY shall provide the Fire Marshal of
PERFORMING PARTY a map of his/her city/town with
streets marked for Identification. It ls expressly
understood between the parties that the County shall
have no

6. General Provisions:

The Parties agree to comply fully with all applicable federal, state,
and local statutes, ordinances, rules, and regulations in connection with
the services contemplated under this Agreement. This Agreement is

subject to all applicable present and future valid laws governing the
same.

This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of Texas,
and venue shall be in Grimes County, Texas.

This Agreement contains the entire agreement of the Parties hereto
with respect to the matters covered by its terms, and it may not be

modified in any manner without the express written consent of the
parties.

If any term(s) or provision(s) of this Agreement are held by a court of
competent jurisdiction to be invalid, void, or unenforceable, the

remainder of the provisions of this Agreement shall remain in full force
and effect.

12.  Human Resources, IT, Elections 
     Grimes County Human Resource Director Atina Adair: Open Enrollment will begin on Wednesday, July 
     22nd. at 7:00 am, located in the Commissioners Courtroom. All vendors will be present to discuss all benefits 
     they have to offer. Employees do not have to make an election on that day. Employees will have the 22nd to 
     make an election or they have until July 29th by 2:00 pm in person in the HR Office to let HR know how they 
     want to move forward with their benefits. Current vacancies in the county: Jobs are being sourced thru current 
     Job Boards such as Work in Texas.com which is through Texas Workforce, Ameritech Temp Agency using 
     for Road Department. In the Sheriff's office there are 4 patrol positions vacant, 2 Jailers and 1 Dispatcher. 
     Road Dept needs 2 light equipment operators. Solid waste needs 1 part time dump site attendant. Auditors 
     office has an Assistant Auditor 1 position. Maintenance has a full time position with the facilities 
     maintenance crew.  
     Grimes County IT Director Gregg Cannon: Systems have been up 99.8%. No cybersecurity incidents and no      major outages. Approximately 26,000 phishing attempts with zero successful. 95% patch compliant. One 
     backup system that is not operating in a timely fashion. Working with the Engineers of the company to try to 
     resolve the issue. Storage capacity is still growing. Currently at 71.5%. The system in the elevator at the 
     Historical Courthouse is being updated. The company that is installing the new system upgrade inadvertently 
     cut the video camera feed for that elevator and are in the process of repairing it. The new phone system in the 
     elevator has been tested. There is new regulations for ADA compliance that the phone has to be able to call 
     and visually see that the call was made. By pressing a button, it will automatically dial out to 911 Dispatch. 
     Help desk received 182 tickets and closed 173. IT is working with Dept heads to make sure they have 
     everything for upcoming budget hearings. New AI training course that will be part of the grant process, 
     notifications will be going out. Judge Fauth wanted to re-visit the storage capacity issue. The Judge asked if 
     there was a report that could be run to identify duplicate items. Mr Cannon stated that some reports have 
     identified 200,000 duplicates. IT is looking into storing data on tape as opposed to cloud or disc. Precint 1 
     Commissioner Mallett asked if there are best practices or procedures to ensure the user does not have 
     duplicates. Mr Cannon stated there are things that can be done like setting quotas for departments allotting so 
     much storage and if the exceed that amount, it will not allow them to file save. Mr Cannon stated that the 
     Sheriff's office and the DA's office are doing a good job of managing files to alleviate duplicates.  
     Grimes County Elections Administrator Rachel Walker: 21,810 Registered Voters, 314 DPS Applications, 
     137 mail in applications, 5 walk in applications, 11 Applications Ballot by mail-RCVD, 5 Applications Ballot 
     by mail-mailed, 44 Notice of Address confirmations, 5 Notice of Examination, 28 Notice of Incomplete, 4 
     Notice of Cancellation, 2 Notice of Rejection, 11 Address Confirmation Responses, 2 Examination 
     Responses, 7 Undeliverable by Post Office, 1 VR Card Undeliverable by Post Office, 4 Jury Reports, 11 
     Death Abstracts Reports, 80 Statements of Residence, 2 Open Records Requests, 26 Total Certified VDR's. 
     Another issue with TEAM related to voter registration processing. The system was unable to properly match 
     DPS applications to already existing registered voters which could result in duplicate records. Additionally 
     some DPS applications imported into the system were missing signatures or dates. For applications 
     transferred from another county, staff had to contact the previous county to request the most recent signature 
     on file. All of this resulted in time consuming work-arounds. In May and June the Elections office completed 
     post-election work for the May 2nd City and School Election, administered the May 26th run-off election, 
     completed required audits, prepared FY 27 budget, monitored Legislative activity related to election charges, 
     advanced courthouse event scheduling procedures, prepared a voter registration outreach event, attended State 
     training related to SB 2753 implementation and Risk-Limiting Audits.May 2nd election produced and 
     distributed the Official Election Results for all 4 local entities and completing the Post Election Hand Count 
     Audit (PEHCA). This covered the entire May 2nd election including all races and all polling locations. Staff 
     hand counted all 925 ballots cast. The PEHCA results reflected that hand-count totals matched the 
     electronically tabulate totals for the audited ballot groups shown. May 26th Primary Runoff Election. Staffed 
     the election with 52 election workers covering 135 shifts. Operated 9 total polling locations including 2 early 
     voting. Maintained polls open for a cumulative total of 72 hours. Recorded 3,484 voters participating for 
     16.08% overall turnout, 3,202 Republican voters representing 14.78% turnout, 264 Democratic voters 
     representing 1.22% turnout. 3,466 ballots counted. Rejected 18 ballots including 15 ballot by mail and 3 
     provisional with an overall rejection rate of 0.52% of ballots cast. Used 3 counting teams with 6 staff 
     members to complete the required post-election hand count audit. The Republican races audited were US 
     Senator, Attorney General and County Commissioner Pct 2. The Democratic races audited were Lieutenant 
     Governor, Attorney General, and Justice 14th Court of Appeals District Place 7. The PEHCA results reflected 
     that the hand count totals matched electronically totals for the audited races and ballot groups. Administrative 
     and operational work as follows: Prepared FY 27 budget. Prepared the system to handle scheduling 
     Courthouse events and distributed to eligible parties. Legislative monitoring and training as follows: 
     Monitored developments by watching interim hearings held by the House Elections Committee and the 
     Senate State Affairs Committee. Attended a two-day Secretary of State regional training. Received additional 
     information related to HB 2753 implementation. Participated in an exercise to prepare for th Risk-Limiting 
     Audit scheduled after the November election, which will be the first time this audit has been conducted 
     statewide.

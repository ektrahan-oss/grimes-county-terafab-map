Public Comments
Bob Raley of the Roans Prairie area off Highway 179 addressed the court regarding road conditions on
Highway 30 and access roads to industrial sites. He reported that increased traffic from the TeraFab
facility and heavy trucks has created hazardous conditions, with dirt and mud accumulating on the
pavement and becoming  a safety concern during rain events. Raley cited similar issues at the Republic
Services facility near the county dump and drew on his twenty-five years of transportation safety
experience in the oil industry to recommend that the county coordinate with the Department of
Transportation to require either paved access roads or ground stabilization measures at industrial and
disposal sites to prevent debris from being tracked onto public highways. He concluded by thanking
Commissioner Tullos for his service to the county.

21. Discuss and approve  Master Service Agreement   for Sheriff's office Covert software.
       IT Director Gregg Cannon reported that the sheriff's office Criminal Investigation Division uses
       software to track investigators for officer safety purposes. The department previously used a
       product called Callio but is switching to Covert because it is cheaper and offers features the staff
       prefers. Commissioner Michael Raska asked which agenda item was the county saving
       $24,000? Mr. Cannon  stated it is not on an agenda item today, it is on a storage appliance
       contract. Following the discussion, a question arose regarding contract approval procedures and
       signature authority. County Attorney Megan Barcak clarified that all contracts must be approved
       by Commissioners  Court to bind the county, regardless of dollar amount, and that such approval
       must occur before execution. Barcak explained that her office reviews contracts first, after which
       they are placed on the court agenda for approval before being signed. IT Director Cannon noted
       that the county judge had expressed concern about requiring court approval for very small
       contracts while ensuring larger agreements receive attention, but Barcak confirmed that only
       Commissioners  Court approval binds the county on any contract value, and that the court
       determines who may  sign on its behalf.

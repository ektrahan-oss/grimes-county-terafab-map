Call to Order

County Judge Joe Fauth called the regular meeting of Commissioners Court of Grimes County to
order on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. at 270 FM 149 West, Anderson, Texas,
and reminded attendees that the meeting would be recorded.

Invocation

Pastor Henry Sanders delivered the invocation, expressing gratitude for the day and asking for
divine guidance over the court's decisions. Sanders offered prayers for protection of law
enforcement and first responders, for the county's school systems and their superintendents, and
for the families of the community. He also prayed for the safety of military personnel overseas
and for wisdom among national leaders, and asked that the commissioners be guided to make
decisions that would bring unity to the community.

Pledge of Allegiance to the U.S. Flag
Pledge of Allegiance to the Texas Flag

Public Comments

We look forward to hearing from the citizens of Grimes County. If you wish to make public
comments you will need to submit a Public Participation Form prior to the start of the
meeting and give it to the County Clerk. Public comments will be limited to 2 minutes per
person.

Joseph Reznicek addressed the Commissioners Court with budget concerns, urging the court to
postpone new construction projects including the jail and annex until sufficient funds were
available, arguing that people and services should take priority over building commitments. He
recommended using savings from delayed projects to increase county employee wages
competitively, noting that the cost of recruiting and training replacements for employees who
leave for better-paying positions would exceed the current budget by approximately $1.9 million.
Reznicek also called for comparable pay with state law enforcement agencies to retain
experienced staff and attract new deputies and constables needed for anticipated county growth.
He requested allocation of funds for two additional ambulances with crews and equipment,
support for volunteer emergency services responders, distribution of funds to volunteer fire
departments for life-saving supplies and maintenance, adequate resources for road and bridge
repairs and assessments, and a minimum of two inspection technician positions for the county
engineer's office to manage the increasing volume of permits.

Presentation of Monthly Departmental Reports:

4. Elections, Veterans Services, Emergency Management Services — Elections,
Veterans Services, Emergency Management Services

Veterans Services Coordinator Jay Lafferty reported on two months of activity. In July,
he recorded eighteen office contacts, thirty-six phone contacts, and seven email
contacts, noting that fewer people visited during hot weather and preferring to call
instead. Three new claims were started and one existing claim was updated. Lafferty's
banner was displayed at a gun show where it was seen by over eight hundred people,
and he thanked maintenance for assisting with events to increase visibility of veterans
services. In August, he had thirty-two phone contacts, six office contacts, and five email
contacts. Lafferty completed a required artificial intelligence course for county employees
and processed a claim for submission. Three representatives from the Texas Veterans
Commission visited to explain their reorganized operations and discuss issues affecting
veteran service officers and the services provided by the Texas Veterans Commission,
which approves all of Lafferty's work. County Judge Joe Fauth asked whether the visitors
discussed any legislative priorities to raise with state representatives, and Lafferty
responded that increased staffing at the Texas Veterans Commission would be helpful,
as that agency consistently operates with insufficient personnel. EMC/Fire Marshal
Dione Bumpous reported that since the burn ban was placed on August 17, the
department responded to two significant incidents: an RV vehicle fire that sparked
neighboring properties, prompting Red Cross assistance during a heat advisory, anda
booth fire at the Renn Fair, for which she thanked Montgomery County Fire Marshal's
Office, the ATF, and the Grimes County Sheriff's Office for their investigation assistance.
Bumpous stated that the drought index stood at seven hundred fourteen, rising
approximately one hundred points since the ban was initiated, and she recommended
maintaining the burn ban in place. The department completed two operational permits
involving fire suppression and alarm system inspections. Bumpous reported that Grimes
County received approval for individual assistance related to July 2026 flooding and that
public assistance had been submitted alongside two other counties previously omitted
from the list. Work on emergency indexes and emergency support functions continues,
with completion required by December of the next year.

Consider and take action to approve the Union Grove Baptist Church-Whitehall as an
Election Day Polling Location. — Rachel Walker, Grimes County Elections

Elections Administrator Rachel Walker presented the Union Grove Baptist Church in Whitehall as
a replacement polling location for the Whitehall Community Center, which had contractual
issues. The church is located on Highway 362, is well known in the community, and offers ample
parking with proper handicap accessibility and concrete surfaces—all factors that address audit
findings regarding the previous location. The facility features a fellowship hall separate from the
sanctuary where voting would take place, multiple bathrooms, and adequate space for entry and
exit. Walker met with the church director, Sue Wimberly, who brought the proposal before the
church body, and it was unanimously approved. The church raised initial concerns about
candidate signs but understood after Walker explained that polling locations must remain neutral.
The church did not request a rental fee and expressed willingness to serve the community.
Whitehall typically draws close to two hundred voters on election day, making an active polling
location important for the county. Walker noted that the county had gone without a location in
Whitehall for a couple of recent elections and sought court approval to use Union Grove Baptist
Church for the November election.

15. Request authorization to negotiate a professional services contract with HR Green, Inc for
pipeline permitting and inspection, no cost to Grimes County. — Jon Steiber, Grimes
County Engineer

County Engineer Jon Steiber explained that pipeline construction in Grimes County requires
various permits and inspections covering driveways, floodplain issues, road use, road crossings,
borings, and occasionally site plans for permanent pump or switch stations. Steiber noted that
he, along with other county staff, spent approximately six hundred hours on permitting and
inspection work for the Kinder Morgan pipeline over sixteen months, and that the fees charged
for in-house work do not adequately cover the effort required. He proposed engaging HR Green,
Inc., a construction management firm experienced in pipeline inspections, to conduct inspections
and submit detailed inspection reports for the upcoming Grand Prix pipeline project crossing the
north of the county, thereby protecting county rights of way and roads during construction.
Steiber indicated this arrangement would likely become standard practice for the one to two
pipeline projects the county typically receives annually and recommended approval. County
Attorney Megan Barcak clarified for the record that this item authorizes negotiation of the
contract only and is not approval of the contract itself, with the final contract to be presented to
the court at a later date.

Action: Motion by Commissioner Chad Mallett to Authorize negotiation of a professional services
contract with HR Green, Inc. for pipeline permitting and inspection, at no cost to Grimes County.
Seconded by Commissioner Michael Raska. Carried.

County Auditor Jessi Murphy walked the Commissioners Court through the FY 2027 proposed
budget, presenting two options for TCOLE employee compensation structures. Human Resource
Director Atina Adair explained that Option One would move patrol deputies and deputy
constables from the current year midpoint to the midpoint, then add a four-dollar increase across
the board, while Option Two would apply a four-dollar increase to the current salary structure for
all TCOLE employees and expand the step scale from nine plus to sixteen plus to honor prior
year experience from other agencies. After extensive discussion, County Judge Joe Fauth
initially favored Option One but reconsidered, while Commissioner Tullos and Commissioner
Mallett expressed concern that Option Two, despite providing equal treatment across positions,
might lead the Sheriff's Office to use SB 22 funds to supplement patrol deputy salaries and
create wage disparities. The court took a roll call vote on the judge's motion to adopt Option Two,
which failed 2 Yea and 3 Nay, resulting in the adoption of Option One instead. The court then
addressed numerous detailed budget adjustments, including software and hardware updates for
IT development, corrections to overtime budgets, and position changes across multiple
departments. Significant items included approving Fire Marshal Dione Bumpous's transition
budget for her academy attendance and recommending removal of her conference travel line,
authorizing an additional patrol deputy position to be funded through SB 22 funds, and adjusting
the jail's contract with Southern Health Partners by transferring $4,500 from inmate medical to
the professional services line. The court also grappled with funding for County Engineer Jon
Steiber's road and bridge grant projects, ultimately approving a transfer of $181,008 from Road
and Bridge reserves to cover shortfalls in the Tawneyberry Live Oak grant.

Action: Motion by County Judge Joe Fauth to Approve option two for the TCOLE salary structure
in the FY 2027 budget, adding a $4 per hour increase to the current salary structure for all TCOLE
employees across the grid and expanding the step scale from 9+ to 16+. Seconded by
Commissioner Phillip Cox. Failed (2-3-0).

Record Vote: Voting Yea: County Judge Joe Fauth, Commissioner Phillip Cox
Voting Nay: Commissioner Chad Mallett, Commissioner David Tullos, Commissioner
Michael Raska

Action: Motion by Commissioner Chad Mallett to Approve option one for the TCOLE salary
structure in the FY 2027 budget, moving patrol deputies’ current year midpoint to the min and
adding a $4 per hour increase to the salary structure. Seconded by Commissioner David Tullos.
Carried (3-2-0).

valuation for Grimes County for Tax Year 2026 as follows:

$0.395000 for the purposes of maintenance and operations

$0.000000 for the payment of debt service

$0.395000 total tax rate

County Judge Joe Fauth noted that Chapter 26 of the property tax code requires the
Commissioners Court of Grimes County to adopt a tax rate and requested a motion to accept the
proposed rate of $0.395000 per $100 taxable valuation for maintenance and operations, with
$0.000000 for debt service, for a total tax rate of $0.395000. Commissioner David Tullos moved
the proposed tax rate for adoption. County Judge Joe Fauth then conducted a roll call vote, with
Commissioners Mallett, Tullos, Raska, and Cox each voting affirmatively, as did the County
Judge. After the commissioners signed the necessary documents, County Judge Joe Fauth
thanked the auditors for their work and noted that the adopted tax rate represents the lowest tax
rate for Grimes County since 1990 or 1991. County Attorney Megan Barcak clarified the effective
date in the record to be the 16th day of September, and County Judge Joe Fauth expressed
appreciation to the court for the work involved in reaching this rate, which benefits the citizens of
Grimes County.

Action: Motion by Commissioner David Tullos to Adopt the tax rate for Grimes County for Tax
Year 2026 at $0.3950 per $100 taxable valuation ($0.3950 for maintenance and operations, $0.0000
for debt service), pursuant to Local Government Code Section 111.010. Seconded by
Commissioner Phillip Cox. Carried (5-0-0).

ATTACHMENT A: SPECIFICATIONS
GRIMES COUNTY SOLID WASTE SERVICES

Grimes County operates six Household Solid Waste collection sites in the unincorporated
area providing waste collection and disposal services to County residents and to
discourage illegal dumping.

Grimes County is seeking a Contractor to provide disposal services, leasing services or
both to Grimes County to lease 5 waste compactors and/or haul and dispose of solid
waste collected at each site. Contractor leasing equipment must be able to provide roll-
off containers and equipment necessary for the collection of Household Solid Waste at
each collection site for Grimes County. Contactors hauling and disposing of solid waste
must meet the requirements of these specifications. Contractors may bid on both leasing,
hauling/disposal or may bid individually on leasing and individually on hauling/disposal.

Household Solid Waste includes any garbage and rubbish as defined under Texas Health
and Safety Code 361.003 (Solid Waste Disposal Act) which can lawfully be deposited,
dumped, or placed in any Grimes County collection site. Household Solid Waste does
not include demolition/construction debris, hazardous substances, or industrial solid
waste.

Roll-off containers are the type of solid waste industry container to be provided by the
Contractor and loaded by roll-off trucks.

All roll-off containers and collection equipment shall be placed for service within Grimes
County and at such locations as selected and prepared by Grimes County and its
employees, contractors, or subcontractors. Grimes County shall be solely responsible for
obtaining the rights, license, privileges, easements, or title to the real property on which
each collection site is located. Grimes County shall prepare the site collection site for the
roll-off container and collection equipment, with consultation and advice from the
Contractor. After Grimes County has designated a collection site, the Contractor shall
provide the roll-off container and collection equipment at such site within thirty (30) days
after completion of the necessary site preparation by Grimes County and notice thereof.
During the performance of services, the Contactor shall not place collection containers
on public streets, alleys, or thoroughfares. All vehicles used by the Contractor to remove
and haul solid waste shall be covered to prevent spillage, blowing, or scattering of refuse
onto public streets or private property.

All collections made under the agreement shall be made by the Contractor without
unnecessary noise, disturbance, or interference with Grimes County citizens.

ATTACHMENT A: SPECIFICATIONS
GRIMES COUNTY SOLID WASTE SERVICES

Grimes County operates six Household Solid Waste collection sites in the unincorporated
area providing waste collection and disposal services to County residents and to
discourage illegal dumping.

Grimes County is seeking a Contractor to provide disposal services, leasing services or
both to Grimes County to lease 5 waste compactors and/or haul and dispose of solid
waste collected at each site. Contractor leasing equipment must be able to provide roll-
off containers and equipment necessary for the collection of Household Solid Waste at
each collection site for Grimes County. Contactors hauling and disposing of solid waste
must meet the requirements of these specifications. Contractors may bid on both leasing,
hauling/disposal or may bid individually on leasing and individually on hauling/disposal.

Household Solid Waste includes any garbage and rubbish as defined under Texas Health
and Safety Code 361.003 (Solid Waste Disposal Act) which can lawfully be deposited,
dumped, or placed in any Grimes County collection site. Household Solid Waste does
not include demolition/construction debris, hazardous substances, or industrial solid
waste.

Roll-off containers are the type of solid waste industry container to be provided by the
Contractor and loaded by roll-off trucks.

All roll-off containers and collection equipment shall be placed for service within Grimes
County and at such locations as selected and prepared by Grimes County and its
employees, contractors, or subcontractors. Grimes County shall be solely responsible for
obtaining the rights, license, privileges, easements, or title to the real property on which
each collection site is located. Grimes County shall prepare the site collection site for the
roll-off container and collection equipment, with consultation and advice from the
Contractor. After Grimes County has designated a collection site, the Contractor shall
provide the roll-off container and collection equipment at such site within thirty (30) days
after completion of the necessary site preparation by Grimes County and notice thereof.
During the performance of services, the Contactor shall not place collection containers
on public streets, alleys, or thoroughfares. All vehicles used by the Contractor to remove
and haul solid waste shall be covered to prevent spillage, blowing, or scattering of refuse
onto public streets or private property.

All collections made under the agreement shall be made by the Contractor without
unnecessary noise, disturbance, or interference with Grimes County citizens.

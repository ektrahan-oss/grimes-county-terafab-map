Public Comments

County Judge Joe Fauth asked whether any public comment submissions had been received and
called the registered speakers in the order their forms were received. Sherry Fauth of Plantersville,
precinct chair for Precinct 4 and an election worker since 2008, spoke to answer questions raised at
the previous Commissioners Court meeting regarding the Plantersville voting location. She stated that
the location has never had lines two hours long, that the longest wait she could recall was
approximately forty five minutes during the November election and was caused by machine problems,
and that she was not aware of any voter leaving the location unable to vote other than those who were
not qualified and declined to cast a provisional ballot. She said residents are aware that two locations
are open for early voting and that many voters choose to vote on election day. She also noted that she
founded the Grimes County Republican Women's group in 2011, which has grown from twelve charter
members to more than sixty two members, and that she serves as deputy president for Region 7 of the

Grimes County Judge - Joe Fauth Il

11 Discuss and take action to review the previous consideration of the Commissioners Court
regarding early voting locations for the November election. — Joe Fauth Ill

County Judge Joe Fauth introduced the discussion of early voting locations for the November
election, noting that the court had previously discussed this matter during the budget cycle in
connection with the primary runoff in May. Alec Pointer, chairman of the Republican Party,
presented the Republican County Executive Committee's position on proposed early voting
locations in lola and Plantersville. Pointer detailed the cost analysis the party had conducted,
including rental fees for facilities and staffing expenses, and emphasized that the largest cost
barrier was equipment, estimated at approximately eighty thousand dollars, since early voting
equipment must be separate from election day equipment and counted separately. He raised
concerns about the timing of equipment investment given that all voting equipment would need
replacement within two to four years at a cost exceeding one million dollars, and noted that a
pending state Senate bill would fundamentally change early voting requirements, potentially
making near-term changes inefficient. Pointer stressed that both parties supported investing in
full-time election office staff, technology improvements, and election judge training as higher
priorities than expanding locations. He also noted that a significant portion of Republican voters
prefer election day voting over an extended voting period, though he personally acknowledged that
the party could reconsider if the scope or duration of early voting operations changed.
Commissioner David Tullos then challenged several aspects of Pointer's analysis, citing voter
turnout disparities between precincts with early voting access and those without, noting an eleven
percent turnout difference between Anderson and Todd Mission precincts, and arguing that
offering early voting in Plantersville and lola would ease access for voters in outlying areas like
Todd Mission and Crown Ranch. Tullos contended that early voting expansion need not compete
with other investments and that the court could fund personnel, equipment, and expanded
locations simultaneously. Pointer responded that turnout differences could reflect factors beyond
early voting availability and that statistical analysis should not assume causation from correlation
alone. Tullos then stated that Republican voters he had personally contacted overwhelmingly
supported early voting in Plantersville and expressed frustration that the Republican Party
executive committee had not polled the broader membership before making its decision. Pointer
defended the party's process and the precinct chairs' engagement with their communities,
expressing concern about the level of public discourse in the county and advocating for respectful
dialogue. Elections Administrator Rachel Walker then reported that she needed clarification from
the Secretary of State's office regarding the statutory requirements for opening an additional early
voting location late in the early voting period, suggesting that locations might be able to open for
limited periods if proper notice had been posted earlier. Judge Fauth indicated that the court would
take no action on the agenda item at that time and would revisit the matter during budget
discussions, pending receipt of Walker's clarification on the statutory requirements.

Responsible Development, who presented a petition signed by 894 county residents and

Grimes County Judge - Joe Fauth Il

13 Participate in a question and answer presentation with SpaceX/Terafab representatives. —
Joe Fauth Ill

County Judge Joe Fauth introduced representatives from SpaceX and TeraFab to present
information about the proposed semiconductor manufacturing facility project in Grimes County.
Judge Fauth prefaced the presentation by noting that he had considered removing the item from
the agenda pending approval of the Jetty programs by local school boards, which had ultimately
been approved, allowing the project to move forward. Bucky Brannan, SpaceX's legal
representative specializing in economic development incentives, provided financial context for the
incentive agreements, explaining that Texas imposes relatively high property tax burdens on large
industrial facilities compared to other states and that the legislature has established tax abatement
programs to encourage such development. He detailed the Chapter 312 and Chapter 381
agreements between SpaceX and Grimes County, which fix the property tax obligation at
$20,000,000 annually for thirty-five years, representing a 78% estimated abatement while still
generating $710,000,000 in total payments over that period. Brannan contextualized this by
comparing SpaceX's projected county tax payments of $20,000,000 annually to current payments
from major industrial taxpayers such as CenterPoint, Texas Instruments, Samsung, and Tesla, all
of which pay substantially less to their respective counties. Jordan Buss, SpaceX's environmental,
health, safety, and medical director, discussed the strategic importance of domestic semiconductor
manufacturing for national competitiveness and resilience, emphasized the company's
commitment to environmental stewardship and community integration based on experience at
Starbase in South Texas, and noted that the facility would bring approximately 3,000 permanent
jobs spanning a wide range of skill levels from food service to advanced engineering. Riley Trettel,
the engineer leading TeraFab's development, explained the site selection rationale, including
proximity to population centers within the Texas Triangle, access to water storage through the
Gibbons Creek Reservoir with its 70,000 acre-feet capacity, and connection to the Navasota River
through an existing permit that cannot completely restrict river flow. He detailed plans for on-site
natural gas power generation with advanced emissions controls, battery storage, a 500-foot

GRIMES COUNTY COMMISSIONERS COURT — REGULAR MEETING — WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 5, 2026 Page 7 of 17

setback from neighboring properties, and closed-loop water systems designed to minimize
environmental impact, while clarifying that the 100,000,000 square-foot facility would occupy only
approximately 3,000 of the 13,000 acres under company control. The discussion was substantive
and lengthy, covering multiple technical and financial aspects of the project, with the court and
public raising detailed questions about construction dewatering impacts on private wells, housing
and infrastructure capacity for construction workers, the timing of permanent power plant
operations relative to facility completion, the purpose of the large 22,000-acre reinvestment zone
designation, plans for data centers and aquifer storage, and regulatory oversight of water
resources.

taxes, despite the actual rate remaining the same. Elections Administrator Rachel Walker
confirmed that adding the required statutory language would not significantly increase costs since
it is part of standard ballot programming. County Judge Joe Fauth acknowledged this as an
unfunded mandate by state legislators and thanked Mr. Mock for his explanation.

EXHIBIT A

What We Are Asking Our Officials to Require

Before approving any tax abatement, Chapter 381 grant, reinvestment zone, development
agreement, or other incentive package for a large-scale industrial project, we ask Grimes
County officials to negotiate for strong, written, enforceable terms, including:

1. Independent Legal Counsel Paid Through a Neutral County-
Controlled Funding Process

For any major tax abatement, Chapter 381 grant, reinvestment zone, development
agreement, or incentive negotiation, Grimes County should retain truly independent
outside counsel whose only loyalty is to the County and its residents.

Any outside counsel assisting with incentive negotiations should be selected by the
County, engaged directly by the County, and paid for through a neutral County-controlled
funding process that does not depend on whether a dealis approved or finalized.

If the developer is required to reimburse legal fees, those funds should be paid intoa
r County-controlled account or reimbursement structure that preserves the attorney’s

independence and avoids any appearance that counsel’s payment depends on the

developer, the applicant, or the successful completion of an incentive agreement.

Grimes County residents deserve confidence that any attorney advising the County is free
to recommend stronger terms, delay approval, reject inadequate protections, or walk away
from negotiations if necessary to protect the public interest.

2. Independent Health, Safety, Water, and Infrastructure Review

For projects that may affect residents, roads, water, emergency response, public safety, or
the environment, the County should require company-funded but County-selected
independent review before incentives are approved.

This may include health and safety risk assessments, water-supply and water-balance
studies, traffic and road-impact reviews, emergency response capacity reviews,
wastewater impact reviews, and environmental safeguards.

The County should not rely only on developer-selected information when deciding whether
public benefits should be granted.

3. Water Use and Groundwater Protection

Judge Fauth and Members of the Commissioners Court:

On behalf of Grimes County Citizens for Responsible Development, I respectfully submit the
enclosed petition requesting greater transparency, public participation, and responsible planning
for proposed large-scale industrial developments in Grimes County.

This petition began on March 13, 2026, and concluded on June 3, 2026. During that period, it
received 630 signatures.

The petition respectfully asks local officials and decision-makers to ensure:

e Full transparency regarding all proposed large-scale industrial developments in Grimes
County

e Public meetings and presentations where residents can hear directly from developers and
ask questions

e Careful evaluation of environmental, infrastructure, and community impacts

e Responsible planning that protects the long-term interests of Grimes County residents

The residents who signed this petition asked for meaningful access to information before major
decisions were made and for opportunities to hear directly from project representatives, ask
questions, and better understand how proposed developments could affect their homes, property,
roads, water, public services, and rural communities.

The petition also reflects a broader request that major industrial development decisions be
approached carefully, openly, and with full consideration of both immediate and long-term
impacts on Grimes County.

WHEREAS, the Texas Legislature created the 12th District Court for Walker, Grimes, and Madison
Counties; and

WHEREAS, the parties recognize that the creation of the 12th District Court requires the combined efforts
of the parties to provide for the salaries, benefits, travel, training, and related expenses, including court

management software, for court personnel and offices; and

WHEREAS, Walker, Grimes, and Madison have determined that it is in the best interest of the public that

paid through Walker County, in accordance with the Walker County Personnel Policy.
3. Costs for travel, training, and related expenses of the 12th District Court Administrator

and Coordinator will be paid through Walker County, in accordance with the Walker County
Personnel Policy.

4. Grimes and Madison will allocate, during their respective budgetary processes, funding
for a proportionate share of the cost for salaries, benefits, travel, training, and related expenses,
including court management software, for the 12th District Court Administrator and Coordinator.
5. The parties agree that a reasonably proportionate share of the costs of Grimes County
and Madison County is equivalent to the proportion of the population of each of the two counties
to the total population of the three counties contained within the 12th District Court. Such
proportionate share for Grimes is 25.89%. Such proportionate share for Madison is 10.75%.
Walker's percentage of the population of the three counties is 63.36%.

6. Grimes and Madison will remit their share of the cost for salaries, benefits, travel,
training, and related expenses for the 12th District Court Administrator and Coordinator invoiced
by Walker.

MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING

Texas Statewide Interoperability Channel Plan (TSICP)
V24.2 (Original issue, April 2005)

Texas Department of Public Safety

and the identified Federal Agency, State Agency, Local Jurisdiction, or Emergency Service
Organization

Purpose

This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) establishes permissions and guidelines for use of
interoperability or mutual aid radio channels by:

e¢ Local government jurisdictions and their associated emergency response agencies;
e State agencies in Texas and their associated emergency response organizations;
e Federal agency local units in Texas and their associated emergency response organizations;

e Local agency units in Texas and their associated emergency response organizations to use
designated Federal interoperability channels, and;

e Private sector emergency response organizations licensed or otherwise entitled to operate in the
Public Safety Pool as defined in Federal Communication Commission (FCC) Rules, Part 90
(47CFR, subpart B, paragraphs 90.15-90.20).

It imposes certain protocols, procedures, and obligations upon jurisdictions hereby authorized to use
state-licensed radio channels held by the Texas Department of Public Safety (TxDPS).

This agreement supersedes any other previous versions of the MOU.

Authority

Execution of this agreement by state and local entities is authorized by Texas Government Code, Chapter
791 (local governments), Chapter 771 (state agencies), and Texas Government Code Chapter 421.096
(Interoperability of Radio Systems). This MOU satisfies FCC Part 90 rules for extending license privileges
to others by agreement.

Federal agencies are permitted access to interoperability channels as authorized by the National
Communications & Information Administration (NTIA) Manual, 47 CFR, Parts 2.102(c), 2.103; and 7.12.
Federal agencies may execute this MOU and shall adhere to the attached guidelines.

Applicability

This MOU authorizes the use of certain radio frequencies by emergency response organizations as
defined by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security’s Emergency Communications Division and the
Texas Department of Public Safety. Generally, this includes organizations in the following governmental

disciplines:
Emergency Management Public Safety Communications
Law Enforcement Public Health
Fire Service Health Care
Emergency Medical Services Hazardous Materials
Public Works / Transportation Governmental Administration

This MOU authorizes use of state-licensed frequencies for the purpose of coordination between
emergency response agencies and resources. Such coordination may occur during interagency
operations, en route travel, or on-incident communications in accordance with an_ Incident
Communications Plan.

Background

The Texas 77* Legislature, in an effort to provide for effective emergency radio communications by state
agencies, called for an Interagency Radio Work Group (IRWG) to develop a state agency
communications network. That group developed a preliminary plan that was accepted by the state IRWG
and the Sheriffs’ Association of Texas on March 27, 2001.

Subsequently, the IRWG determined that the state agency communications network should be expanded
to include all public safety agencies in the state. This was accomplished by IRWG’s development of the
IRCIP of January 2003.

In response to an FCC requirement for establishment of state / regional advisory committees, the Texas
Interoperable Communications Coalition (TxICC) and the Texas Statewide Interoperable
Communications Plan Executive Committee (SEC) were formally established as advisory committees to
TxDPS.

The Texas Statewide Interoperability Channel Plan (TSICP), developed by the TxICC and included in
this MOU, provides essential guidance for interoperable radio communications using VHF, UHF, 700

MHz, 800 MHz, and mobile satellite radio equipment for interagency coordination, en route travel, or on-
incident communications.

e Manage and maintain proper licenses for the use of the interoperability frequencies identified
herein;
e Manage and maintain an accurate database of federal and state agencies and local government
jurisdictions that have accepted and signed this MOU, and;
e Issue updates and revisions to the TSICP contained herein, upon request by the TxICC and the
Texas Statewide Interoperability Coordinator.

                 GRIMES COUNTY COMMISSIONERS COURT
                OFFICIAL MINUTES — WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 9, 2026

The Grimes County Commissioners Court met on Wednesday, September 9, 2026, at 9:00 A.M. Members
Present: County Judge Joe Fauth, Commissioner Chad Mallett, Commissioner David Tullos,
Commissioner Michael Raska, Commissioner Phillip Cox.

Call to Order
  County Judge Joe Fauth called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m. on Wednesday, September 9, 2026,
  in Commissioners Court at 270 FM 149 West Anderson, Texas, noting that notice of the regular
  meeting had been posted in accordance with statute and that all court members were in attendance.
Public Comments
  We look forward to hearing from the citizens of Grimes County. If you wish to make public comments
  you will need to submit a Public Participation Form prior to the start of the meeting and give it to the
  County Clerk. Public comments will be limited to 2 minutes per person.
  The Judge announced that public comments would be limited to two minutes per person and that two
  submissions had been received. Identifying herself as Debra Morwood from Iola, spoke in strong
  support of adding staff to the road and bridge development services, including a development
  technician and administrative position. She emphasized that the permitting, inspections, and
  enforcement functions of the development services department were critical tools for protecting existing
  residents, businesses, and infrastructure as the county experienced growth, and urged the court to
  approve the additional positions and resources requested. Marie Egyed, representing Grimes County
  Citizens for Responsible Development and a resident of Iola, raised concerns about transparency in
  the budget process, noting that the proposed budget available to the public had not matched the
  version being discussed by the court and that revisions had not been reposted with adequate notice.
  She referenced changes to Texas law effective September 1, 2025, requiring that proposed budgets be
  included with meeting notice or clearly available from the website homepage, and requested that
  supporting documents for significant agenda items be made available with sufficient time for public
  review.

Consider and take action regarding the Burn Ban and authorize the County Judge as signatory. —
Joe Fauth III, Grimes County Judge
  Current Burn Ban ordered August 19, 2026, expiring November 17, 2026.
  County Judge Joe Fauth asked EMC/Fire Marshal Dione Bumpous about the status of the burn ban,
  which was ordered August 19, 2026, and expires November 17, 2026. EMC/Fire Marshal Dione
  Bumpous reported that despite recent showers throughout the county, the drought index remained at
  685, which is higher than when the ban was placed, indicating continued high risk and low moisture
  conditions. EMC/Fire Marshal Dione Bumpous recommended no action at this time, and County Judge
  Joe Fauth indicated the court's approval of that recommendation.
  Action: No action.
Discuss and take action on any issue regarding FY 27 Proposed Budget. — Joe Fauth III, Grimes
County Judge
  County Judge Joe Fauth opened discussion of the FY 2027 proposed budget and asked County
  Auditor Jessi Murphy to identify any items requiring particular attention. County Auditor Murphy
  directed the court to Fund 45 on page 52, noting that the Law Enforcement Officers line for the sheriff's
  office had been reduced from $15,000 to $13,007.48 due to remaining reserves, though she noted the
  court could budget the full $15,000 if it preferred to transfer from general funds. She also flagged a line
  item on page 13 that currently budgeted at $51,400 would need to be increased to $56,050 to cover
  contracts in place, explaining that a single audit threshold would be met this year necessitating the
  increase. County Auditor Murphy reported that revenues had been reassessed overall, and she
  directed attention to page 56, line 4274505 for machinery and equipment, where the department head
  had requested an increase to $15,000 for contracted elections, though that line was currently zero.
  County Judge Fauth requested an adjustment to the postage line on page 6, noting that individual
  grievance committee mail packets had increased to $21 each for registered mail, and he asked that the
  commissioners and auditor increase that line by $210 to cover ten packets, raising the total from $150
  to $360. The discussion then turned to the chaplain position for the jail, with Commissioner Phillip Cox
  noting it should be included. County Judge Fauth proposed budgeting it as a temporary position at $15
  per hour for eight hours per week (416 total hours annually) to test the arrangement before potentially
  converting it to part-time status in the following year. County Auditor Jessi Murphy noted the position
  would cost approximately $9,000 to $10,000 depending on classification. The court discussed at length

  One versus Option Two of the proposed pay scale, technical updates to the sheriff's office salary sheet
  provided late in the process, the appropriate minimum wage floor across county positions, and funding
  allocations toward jail infrastructure planning. Commissioner Chad Mallett proposed keeping deputy
  constables at the current minimum salary until a comprehensive salary study could be completed in the
  following year, while Commissioner David Tullos argued for bringing deputy constables to the same
  pay scale as sheriff's deputies to address pay inequity among law enforcement officers doing similar
  work. Precinct 1 Constable James Ellis and Blake Jarvis, Pct 2, representing the constable's precincts,
  testified that constables perform the same law enforcement duties and face the same risks as sheriff's
  deputies and urged pay parity; County Auditor Murphy and Human Resource Director Atina Adair
  provided technical salary information showing an $83 annual difference at the minimum level and
  explaining the impact of Senate Bill 22 funding on the sheriff's office but not the constables' budget.
  County Judge Fauth ultimately indicated that the proposed budget should include option one, which
  brings both deputy constables and sheriff's deputies to the midpoint salary, and instructed the auditor to
  incorporate this decision along with all other discussed adjustments into a revised budget to be
  presented at the following week's meeting.

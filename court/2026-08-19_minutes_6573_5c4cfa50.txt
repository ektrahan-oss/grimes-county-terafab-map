    9  Road Department Report. — Jon Steiber, Road Department
       County Engineer Jon Steiber reported on the road department's standard work and specific
       projects completed. He noted that all washouts from the July 14 flooding have been repaired, and
       County Road 146 bridge was rebuilt using Gabion baskets for a more permanent solution than
       previous dirt-dumping methods. The department completed first course chip sealing on County
       Roads 206, 313, and 316A, is currently stabilizing and chip sealing the remainder of County Road

       court. For development activities, he noted that weekly development training continues on
       Tuesdays at 1PM at the Justice Center Annex, ongoing OSSF maintenance contract compliance
       operations are issuing violations and referring non-compliant parties to the district attorney, and
       OSSF development training is scheduled on dates listed in the report. For grants, County Engineer
       Steiber highlighted that bids for County Road 409 at Tawneyberry will open September 3, and
       property acquisition negotiations are nearing completion pending questions from commissioners'
       court. County Judge Joe Fauth asked whether mowing and shredding work was performed
       in-house or by outside personnel, and County Engineer Steiber explained that a contractor
       performs three to four rounds of mowing annually depending on available funding, while county
       personnel handle requested areas and spot mowing as needed, with sufficient funding available
       for the current year.

Grimes County Commissioner Precinct 4 - Phillip Cox
    12  Discuss and take action to issue a letter of intent to purchase ambulances. — Phillip Cox
       Commissioner Phillip Cox moved that the county issue a letter of intent to purchase four additional
       ambulances, noting that the need for additional ambulance services had been demonstrated
       through data shared with the court multiple times and that ambulances from other counties were
       being called in almost daily because the county's existing fleet was insufficient. Billy Rice was
       invited to assist in the discussion and explained the process for ordering ambulances, clarifying
       that the timeline from letter of intent to first payment would be approximately one year or more,
       with deliveries expected between September and November for ambulances previously ordered.
       County Judge Joe Fauth expressed concerns about the county's ability to absorb the financial
       costs, noting that Saint Joe's Hospital had identified a 1.3 million dollar annual deficit from
       providing ambulance services in Grimes County, with revenues covering only about 50 percent of
       operational costs due to inadequate Medicare and Medicaid reimbursement rates and uninsured or
       underinsured patients. Judge Fauth suggested an alternative approach of phasing in additional
       ambulances over time—keeping two of the older units in service when the two previously ordered
       ambulances arrived rather than replacing all four immediately, allowing the county to eventually
       operate six ambulances by 2028 without incurring all costs at once. Billy Rice confirmed this
       approach was feasible but noted it would increase the cost per mile on older equipment, though he
       emphasized the county's maintenance program could sustain older vehicles through engine and
       transmission rebuilds if necessary. Commissioner David Tullos moved to amend the motion to
       order two ambulances at this time rather than four, with the option to issue another letter of intent
       after the budget was finalized in October or November, and Commissioner Chad Mallett seconded
       the amendment. The discussion at length addressed the relationship between ambulance
       purchases and the county's future subsidy obligations to support EMS services, with recognition
       that adding ambulances would increase the annual deficit the county would need to help cover.

Grimes County Judge - Joe Fauth III
    13  Consider and take action regarding the Burn Ban and authorize the County Judge as
       signatory. — Joe Fauth III
       Not currently under a burn ban order.
       Regarding the proposed burn ban, EMC/Fire Marshal Bumpous conducted a survey of county fire
       chiefs and reported that three recommended implementing a ban, two recommended against it,
       and four were indifferent. She noted that current conditions support a ban, citing a KBDI drought
       index of 621, which exceeds the historical threshold of 600, and that wind and drying conditions
       are worsening. The current burn ban includes four standard exemptions: firefighter training, public
       utility and mining operations, agricultural crop planning or harvesting, and prescribed burns by
       certified managers. EMC/Fire Marshal Bumpous recommended adding an exemption for cooking
       fires in grills and closed cooking appliances such as smoke pits, provided they are conducted on
       dirt, concrete, or rock surfaces with water sources or fire extinguishers available nearby. County
       Judge Joe Fauth expressed initial concern about this exemption given an upcoming fundraiser at
       the fairgrounds but was satisfied upon learning that the facility would comply with safety
       requirements. Commissioner David Tullos questioned whether any existing operations would fall
       under the certified burn manager exemption and was informed that none currently do, though
       some land-clearing operations in the county would be prohibited under the ban if enacted. County
       Judge Joe Fauth moved to initiate a burn ban effective immediately and to exempt cooking fires
       conducted on appropriate surfaces with proper extinguishing materials present, which was
       seconded by Commissioner Chad Mallett. EMC/Fire Marshal Bumpous stated she would publish
       guidelines for the new exemption online, and the motion carried unanimously.
       County Judge Joe Fauth reported that a burn ban had been initiated effective that date and would
       run for ninety days or until further notice by statute, with the option to extend for an additional
       period if needed at the end of ninety days.
       Action: Motion by County Judge Joe Fauth to Authorize a burn ban effective this date, with
       an exemption for cooking fires (barbecue pits, grills, closed cooking appliances)
       conducted on dirt or concrete surfaces with proper extinguishing materi. Seconded by
       Commissioner Chad Mallett. Carried (5–0–0).

    14  Discuss and take action on any issue regarding FY 27 Proposed Budget. — Joe Fauth III
       The court discussed the FY 2027 proposed budget, which had been filed with the county clerk the


GRIMES COUNTY COMMISSIONERS COURT — REGULAR MEETING — WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 19, 2026         Page 5 of 8       previous Friday. County Judge Joe Fauth reported that the proposed tax rate for 2027 had been
       lowered to 0395, marking the lowest tax rate for Grimes County since 1991. Judge Fauth also
       noted a recent adjustment following his discussion with the city manager of Navasota regarding
       the fire department's funding request; the request had been reduced from $175,000 to $140,000,
       and County Auditor Jessi Murphy was asked to incorporate this change into the budget. The court
       then engaged in detailed discussion of several personnel and compensation matters. Human
       Resource Director Atina Adair clarified that when the court previously approved moving patrol
       deputy entry-level positions to the midrange salary, that adjustment should have been applied to all
       similarly classified positions including jail deputies, animal control, and SROs, and she confirmed
       that revised salary structures would be printed for the court's review. The court also discussed the
       District Attorney's investigator compensation, agreeing to maintain a $5,408 stipend for one
       investigator who performs additional duties beyond the scope of a regular investigator position. A
       significant discussion followed regarding EMC and Fire Marshal Dione Bumpous's compensation
       structure. Human Resource Director Atina Adair noted that Bumpous's salary would be lower than
       that of Chief Deputy Martha Smith even when combined with her stipend for fire marshal duties,
       and Commissioner David Tullos recommended revisiting her job description to emphasize her
       expanding fire marshal role and adjusting her salary accordingly, with a portion funded from
       restricted fire marshal funds. After discussion, the court tentatively agreed that approximately 60
       percent of Bumpous's duties were fire marshal-related and 40 percent emergency management,
       though County Judge Fauth deferred finalizing specific salary allocations until the court could
       review detailed numbers at a follow-up meeting scheduled for the following Wednesday. County
       Auditor Jessi Murphy also raised the matter of Bumpous's future TCOLE certification, which is
       planned for spring 2027, and the court agreed that she would receive intermediate-level
       certification pay similar to other TCOLE-certified personnel, with at least 60 percent of that
       increase allocated to her fire marshal duties. The court briefly addressed the proposed purchase of
       four ambulances, which would be funded from reserves. County Auditor Jessi Murphy requested
       clarification on salary increase allocations for the upcoming budget cycle, noting that the budget
       currently included $4 per hour across the board in combined COLA and merit increases, though
       actual distributions would be determined by department managers. Judge Fauth confirmed that for
       elected officials, the increase had been set at $3 per hour. The court also briefly discussed the
       juvenile budget, which begins September 1 and must be entered into the system before month's
       end. Commissioner Chad Mallett expressed concern that the court needed to consider making
       cuts to other areas and increase capital project funding to offset anticipated future expenses and
       reduce the tax burden on citizens. The court deferred further budget discussion until the following
       Wednesday to allow time for detailed financial projections and salary structure revisions to be
       prepared.

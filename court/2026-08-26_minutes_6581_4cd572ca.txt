Grimes County Treasurer - Tom Maynard (JF)
    1  Review and take action regarding a missing receipt on an expense report submitted to
      Accounts Payable. — Tom Maynard, Grimes County Treasurer
       The county treasurer Tom Maynard, explained the process for handling expense reports with
       receipts, noting that occasionally receipts fail to scan properly, particularly narrow restaurant
       receipts that can stick together and disappear during the scanning process. Mr. Maynard stated
       that in this instance, an employee's receipt was distinctly remembered by staff as being present
       but did not appear in the scanned backup documentation, and requested payment with the
       employee held accountable. Jessi Murphy clarified that Accounts Payable's responsibility is to
       ensure compliance with county policy by verifying coding and funding availability, and that because
       no backup documentation was present when the report arrived at their office, they could not verify
       the expense and brought the matter before the court. The disputed amount was $16. Phillip Cox
       moved to approve payment. Joe Fauth raised concern about the cost-benefit of convening the
       court at over $400 per hour to address a $16 discrepancy and asked whether a notarized
       statement from the county treasurer could substitute for the missing receipt. Jessi Murphy
       responded that the policy, adopted by the commissioners court, requires backup receipts and that
       any change to this requirement would need to come through policy revision. Chad Mallett
       suggested that original scans be retained for a period of time to prevent similar issues in the
       future. Joe Fauth reiterated his concern about the expense of the court process relative to the
       amount in question but acknowledged that Jessi Murphy was doing her job and that policy
       compliance required the matter to be brought before the court. The motion to approve by Phillip
       Cox. Seconded by Michael Raska. Carried (5-0-0)

    3  Present information to the Court regarding the 2026 Grimes County Voter Sticker Design
      Contest. — Rachel Walker, Grimes County Elections Administrator
       Rachel Walker presented information about the 2026 Grimes County Voter Sticker Design
       Contest, which is being held every two years and marks the second iteration of the program. She
       reported that the first contest generated overwhelming citizen engagement and positive public
       feedback. The contest is accepting entries until September 9, after which a panel of judges will
       evaluate the submissions and announce winners on National Voter Registration Day. Both
       first-place winners' designs will be created as stickers distributed to citizens at the polls. Rachel
       Walker noted two changes for this year's contest: the I Voted sticker outline will now feature the
       shape of Grimes County instead of a circle, allowing children to create art within the county's
       boundaries, and facilities director Rodney Floyd suggested displaying the first-place winner on the
       side of the new election trailer to showcase it throughout the county. Rachel Walker indicated that
       entry forms and packets are available in her office for citizens without printers, and she confirmed
       that all Grimes County school district superintendents, principals, assistant principals, and art
       teachers have been notified and provided stacks of materials to distribute to students.
    4  Consider and take action to approve the purchase of additional technology equipment for
      the Elections Department using Election Contract Surplus Funds. — Rachel Walker, Grimes
      County Elections Administrator
       Rachel Walker presented a request to purchase two Microsoft Surface tablets for the Elections
       Department using election contract surplus funds in the amount of $3,900. Walker explained that
       the tablets would expand technology capabilities in the office and enhance support provided on
       election day, particularly to improve communication between the Elections Department and polling
       locations. She noted the tablets would enable multiple workstations for temporary staff to handle
       voter inquiries and polling location calls, and would strengthen technology support year-round.
       Walker indicated the purchase would be funded through surplus funds collected from contracted
       elections rather than the general fund, which are approved expenses from Fund 55. Chad Mallett
       moved to approve the request, and Joe Fauth seconded the motion. Carried (5-0-0) The Auditor
       requested to have Ms Walker come back to advise her office and the Court where the funds will
       come from to cover the purchase. Ms Walker stated that whatever line items have money
       available can be put into line item 4505 to cover the $3,900. Commissioner Tullos asked Ms
       Walker if she was sure that she will not be needing any of the money in other line items before the
       end of September. Ms Walker answered she would not.
       Action: Commissioner Mallett amended his motion to include the funding and allow the
       Auditor to make the necessary changes. Judge Fauth senconded. Carried (5-0-0)

GRIMES COUNTY COMMISSIONERS COURT — REGULAR MEETING — WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 26, 2026         Page 2 of 5    5  Discuss and take action on any issue regarding FY 27 Proposed Budget. — Joe Fauth III,
      Grimes County Judge
       The court discussed the FY 2027 proposed budget at considerable length, addressing numerous
       line items and departmental requests. Jessi Murphy presented grand totals reflecting several
       changes made since the initial proposal, including a reduction in one line item from $43,143.54 to
       $140,000, adjustments to DA investigator positions for correct tenure and grade, the addition of jail
       deputies and animal control positions under a new law enforcement structure totaling
       approximately $24,000, and the reinstatement of investigators' stipends under SB 22 for a $6,300
       change. Atina Adair presented merit increase parameters recommending that the $2 merit be tied
       to a five-point performance rating system, with employees rated three receiving 50–75% of the
       allotted merit, fours receiving 75–100%, and fives receiving 100%, while those rated two would be
       placed on performance improvement plans. Following extensive discussion on the EMC fire
       marshal position, the court approved an 85–15 funding split between the fire marshal restricted
       fund and the EMC fund and determined to address the position title prior to budget adoption. The
       court also decided to add a certificate pay line for the fire marshal position and approved changing
       the law enforcement deputy structure from four patrol deputies to three patrol deputies and one
       CID investigator, funded through SB 22 resources. On step-and-grade implementation, the court
       directed that step increases take effect at the beginning of a payroll period rather than mid-year to
       reduce administrative burden, with the auditor and HR to establish parameters. The court allocated
       $8,160.61 in remaining SB 22 funds to help offset the cost of two ADA positions budgeted at
       $130,000 each. Regarding capital projects, the court budgeted $2,350,000 in expenditures,
       comprising $1,500,000 for capital improvements and $850,000 for architectural design of a
       potential jail renovation or rebuild following discovery of structural deficiencies; additionally, the
       court transferred $9.6 million to fund 49 reserves specifically for jail renovation contingency and
       allocated $1.5 million for Precinct 1 renovations. Following Commissioner David Tullos's request to
       identify areas for budget reduction given the $7 million increase over the prior year, the court
       agreed to reduce Road and Bridge new paved road miles from fifteen to ten miles while
       maintaining third seal work on existing roads and directing the department to explore additional
       stabilization of unpaved roads within the reduced budget, and agreed to defer the Road and Bridge
       development technician position to a future year while retaining the admin assistant position. Chad
       Mallett made a motion to increase the salary scale maximum for two department heads in IT and
       the Road Department by 3% to keep them within structure, with an agreement to conduct a
       comprehensive pay scale evaluation in the coming year. Seconded by Joe Fauth. Carried (4-0-1)
    6  Consider and take action to establish the salary, expenses, and other allowances of elected
      County or precinct officers according to Local Government Code 152.011 and 152.013 for
      FY 2027. — Joe Fauth III, Grimes County Judge
       Joe Fauth presented the proposed budget for establishing salaries, expenses, and allowances for
       elected county and precinct officers for fiscal year 2027, noting that he had initially proposed a $4
       per hour increase for all elected officials but was reconsidering a reduction to $3 per hour. Chad
       Mallett stated that elected officials should receive only the cost of living adjustment (COLA) and
       that any additional increases should be based on a comprehensive study, noting that the county's
       current salaries were at or above averages for similar counties. Joe Fauth indicated that his
       numbers were based on comparisons with 21 similar counties to Grimes County. After discussion,
       Chad Mallett clarified his position that a COLA-only increase should be implemented for the
       current budget year, with additional studies conducted later to evaluate further salary adjustments.
       Joe Fauth calculated that a $2 COLA increase would result in an additional $4,160 plus benefits
       for elected officials. Motion by Chad Mallett to establish the salary with a $2 per hour increase for

Public Comments

We look forward to hearing from the citizens of Grimes County. If you wish to make public comments, you will need
to submit a Public Participation Form prior to the start of the meeting and give it to the County Clerk. Public
comments will be limited to 2 minutes per person.

Cointy Judge Joe Fauth stated that the rules of the Court have been presented, posted and available since last year,
that the Court only dedicates 16 minutes to Public Comments. According to the rules, time can be extended.
Precinct 1 Commissioner Malllett suggested to extend the extra 10 minutes.

Public Speakers include: Wilma Kay Peavy, Grimes County Democratic Party, Lee Dillenbeck, Nancy Knudson,
Gardner Brown, Josh Elliott, Joseph Reznicek, Heather Butaud. All expressed concerns of the possibility of a Data
Center in Grimes County. The amount of water consumption, the effects on the surrounding properties, strain on the
power grid and property values.

Additional Speakers include: Grimes County Citizens for Responsible Development. Harry S Trumble, Shiara
Trumble, Glenda Deweese, Marie Egyed and Donna O'Connor. All expressed concerns regarding large scale
industrial projects such as battery storage facilities, solar farms and data centers. Possible effects are water sources,
electricity, infrastructure, property values and health concerns.

Last Speaker is Karen Shiver. Grimes County Pct 7 Chair Democratic Party and all residents of Grimes County. A

second informational meeting will be held in Plantersville on Tuesday April 7th, Plantersville Town Hall. Judge
Fauth reminded everyone that during Public Comments the Judge and Commissioners do not respond but listen
carefully to all comments in order to research their concerns.

PROCLAMATION

Commemorating the 180th Anniversary of the Creation of Grimes County

WHEREAS, Texas transitioned from an independent republic to the 28" state of the United States,

with a formal transfer ceremony on February 19, 1846; and,

WHEREAS, Montgomery County, which had been created out of the Washington Municipality in
1837, at the time covered lands that would become parts of what are now Grimes, Walker, San
Jacinto, Madison, and Waller counties; and,

WHEREAS, the area was part of a region experiencing growth in agriculture, specifically cotton and
corn, relying on both free and slave labor in early statehood and residents petitioned for the creation
of a new county; and,

WHEREAS, on April 6, 1846, the first state legislature accepted the petition and established Grimes
County, carved from the northern part of Montgomery County; and,

WHEREAS, the county was named in honor of early pioneer Jesse Grimes (1788-1866), a signer of
the Texas Declaration of Independence and a veteran of the Texas Revolution who lived in the
county; and,

WHEREAS, the county was officially organized on July 15, 1846; and,

WHEREAS, Henry Fanthorp, owner of Fanthorp Inn, donated the land for the county seat, which
was named Anderson after Kenneth Anderson, the last vice president of the Republic of Texas; and,

WHEREAS, the town of Anderson was already settled with Fanthorp Inn nearby serving as a
prominent stagecoach stop and post office (established 1835); and,

WHEREAS, the Robert Raines Chapter National Society Daughters of the American Revolution calls
for a day to observe the historical significance of the establishment of Grimes County in the State of
Texas:

NOW THEREFORE WE, THE COUNTY JUDGE AND THE GRIMES COUNTY COMMISSIONERS,
do hereby proclaim April 6 as a commemoration of Grimes County’s 180" Birthday.
